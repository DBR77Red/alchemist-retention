How is the game structured?

You're a game designer being evaluated — but what does the progression look like?

A

Campaign / Levels

A series of pre-designed challenges unlocked in order. Each level gives you a specific brief ("Casual puzzle game, keep D7 above 20% with ARPU ≥ $1.50") and grades you on pass/fail. Story arc: you start as a junior designer and climb to Game Director.

Pros

  • Clear sense of progression
  • Teachable — each level introduces a new concept
  • Replayable with letter grades

Cons

  • More content to author
  • Less freedom to experiment
B

Sandbox + Score

Free-form play with a live score calculated from your current simulation results. A weighted formula rewards good metrics (D30, LTV, ARPU) and penalizes bad ones (churn, ad overload). A global leaderboard of saved presets shows your best runs.

Pros

  • Immediate feedback on every slider move
  • Total creative freedom
  • Easier to build — no level authoring

Cons

  • Less narrative / motivation to improve
  • Score formula needs careful tuning
C

Challenges + Sandbox

A curated set of unlockable challenge briefs (e.g., "Idle RPG", "Hypercasual", "Gacha") each with specific win conditions. Between challenges, a free sandbox with scoring lets you experiment. Completing challenges unlocks new game archetypes to model.

Pros

  • Structured learning with free exploration
  • Challenges give context; sandbox gives freedom
  • Most game-like feel

Cons

  • Most complex to build
  • Needs both content AND scoring systems
